> what is ARP, TIL

⏺ ARP = Address Resolution Protocol

  It's the "phone book" that translates IP addresses to MAC addresses on your local network!

  Here's how it works:

  1. Your laptop wants to send data to 10.10.42.68
  2. Problem: Ethernet only understands MAC addresses (like 48:65:ee:1e:23:6e), not IP
  addresses
  3. ARP to the rescue: Your laptop broadcasts "Hey everyone! Who has IP 10.10.42.68? Tell me
  your MAC!"
  4. Pknight controller responds: "That's me! My MAC is aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff"
  5. Your laptop caches this in its ARP table and sends packets directly to that MAC

  You can see your current ARP table:
  # Show all ARP entries
  arp -a

  # Show just entries for your subnet
  arp -a | grep "10.10.42"

  # Watch it in action - clear entry then ping
  sudo arp -d 10.10.42.68  # Delete cached entry
  ping -c 1 10.10.42.68     # This triggers ARP request
  arp -a | grep "10.10.42"  # Now you'll see it cached!

  Fun facts:
  - ARP only works on the same subnet (that's why routers exist for different subnets)
  - Entries expire after ~20 minutes (varies by OS)
  - "ARP poisoning" is a common network attack where bad actors lie about their MAC
  - That 169.254.x.x address you keep seeing? Uses ARP too, but only for "link-local"

  This is why same-subnet communication doesn't need a router - ARP handles the MAC address
  discovery directly over the ethernet cable!
